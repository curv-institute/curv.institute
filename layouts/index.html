WHAT WE DO

CURV Institute develops and tests Representational Science, a scientific framework that studies how complex systems encode, transform, and regulate information, and how failures at the representational level give rise to instability, inefficiency, and loss of control.

The framework is meant to stay consistent with established physics. Consistency demonstrations to date are reduced-sector, model-system results, and the bridge from those to the framework's top-level claims remains an open derivation problem that we treat as long-horizon work.

Our current focus is exact structure in finite-state stochastic systems: which asymmetries a partially observed process can and cannot show, at what scale, and with what certificate. Every result is a theorem, a witness that can be checked directly, or a stated open question.

HOW WE WORK

CURV Institute runs its research as a reproducible-science pipeline. Each question is filed as a scoped order, executed under a fixed interface, and returned as a sealed result packet with exact numerical witnesses, content-addressed inputs, and the checks that were run to accept or reject it. Accepted results are replayable from the packet alone.

The same discipline applies to negative results and to confrontations with the literature: a published bound is either recovered, sharpened, or shown to fail on a concrete example, and the example is part of the record.
